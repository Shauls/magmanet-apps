Privacy Policy — I'm OK (אני בסדר)

Last updated: 2026-11-05

I'm OK (אני בסדר) is made by Magmanet. A person taps a button once a day, and their family see it. This policy says exactly what it stores. The short version: a nickname, the times of the taps and a schedule are on the Magmanet server so that the family's phones can see them; only someone with the invite code can join; there is no location, no health data and no phone number on the server; and all of it can be deleted.

The app is not an emergency service or a medical alert. It can't call for help, and it doesn't call or message anyone by itself. Alerts can be late or fail if a phone is off, offline or closes apps in the background. It doesn't replace regular contact.

What is on the server

  • The connection: what the family calls the person (2 to 16 characters; no real name needed), an invite code and when it was issued.
  • The schedule: the time by which a tap is expected, on which weekdays, whether it's paused and until when, when any of that last changed, and the time zone of the person's phone (a name such as Asia/Jerusalem), because "by 11:00" means the person's clock. A time zone is the closest thing here to a place; it says no more than that.
  • Each phone in it: the nickname chosen (2 to 16 characters), a small number within the connection, and whether it's the person's phone or family, and when it joined.
  • Each tap: the date on the person's clock and the time. The first tap of each day. Nothing else is sent with it.

Everyone in a connection sees all of the above. The server doesn't show any of it to anyone else, and looking at an invite code before joining shows only the name and whether the person's phone has joined.

The account is anonymous: the server creates a random user id and access tokens, stored on your phone. The app also stores a random number made at install time, used only to create that account. There is no email, phone number or password.

What the app never collects

Location (no location permission, no map, no tracking), health data of any kind, contacts, messages, call history, photos, payments and advertising identifiers.

The phone number for the call button stays on the phone it was typed into. It is never sent to the server or to anyone. The button opens your phone's dialler with the number filled in, and you decide whether to call. The app makes no calls and sends no messages by itself.

When it is deleted

  • Automatically, every night: the time of a tap, 30 days after it; a whole connection, when nobody has opened it for 120 days.
  • The person's phone leaves, or a family member removes it: all the tap times are erased at once.
  • A family phone leaves or is removed: its nickname goes at once.
  • A family member deletes the connection: everything in it is erased at once.
  • Delete all my data (Settings), or DELETE /v1/profile: this phone leaves every connection as above, and its anonymous account and tokens are deleted.
  • An invite code stops working after 14 days, and a family member can replace it at any time.

Server backups, if any are kept, age out on the server's normal backup rotation.

Anonymous usage statistics

To improve the app it sends anonymous statistics: a random install ID, which screens and features are used and for how long, counts, the app and Android version, phone model, language, and daily counts of error types. Never what anyone typed: no nicknames, no codes, no phone numbers, and no times of taps as such. You can turn this off in Settings.

On your phone

The app keeps a copy of your connections in its private storage, so that it works offline. allowBackup is off, so this copy stays out of device backups. "Copy backup" in Settings copies what's stored as text to your clipboard, including the phone number and the tokens: keep it to yourself.

Notifications are local, scheduled on your phone. Family phones are asked for permission when they're set up; the person's phone only when the reminder is turned on. A family notice shows the name you gave the person, on the lock screen too.

Checks in the background: on Android, the app asks the phone to let it check the server now and then while it's closed. No more is sent than when it's open.

Sharing an invite uses your phone's share sheet: you choose the app and the recipient.

Who else sees it

Nobody. The data isn't sold or shared with any third party and isn't used for advertising. The server writes no access log for this app's requests, and these tables aren't copied to the test server.

Children

The app isn't directed at children.
