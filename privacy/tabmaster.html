Privacy Policy — TabMaster

Last updated: 2026-10-18

TabMaster is made by Magmanet. This policy explains what the app does with your information. The short version: your data stays on your device; the app only sends anonymous usage statistics, which you can turn off.

What we collect

TabMaster has no accounts, no advertising and no tracking. The only thing we collect is anonymous usage statistics (see below), which you can turn off. Apart from that, it goes online only for the update check described under Play nearby below (the Magmanet server, with GitHub Pages as a fallback). We never see, sell or share your data.

Where your data is stored

Your settings, practice history, set lists, imported pieces and the tabs you write are saved only on your device, in the app's private storage. They are removed when you uninstall the app.

The app's Copy backup feature (Settings → Your data) copies your settings, history and set lists as text to your clipboard, so you can keep them somewhere you choose. Where you paste it is up to you; the app never uploads it.

Opening, saving and sharing files

You can import Guitar Pro, MusicXML and text-tab files and export your pieces (for example as MusicXML, text tab or PDF). TabMaster uses Android's own file picker for this, so it only sees the single file you choose, and it needs no storage permission. Imported files are copied into the app's private storage and are never uploaded. When you tap Share, the file goes to the app you pick in the system share sheet.

Microphone

The microphone is used only while you play along with the app listening: play-along with listening on, wait mode, the learn-mode bar checks and a Play nearby duel. The sound is analysed on your device in real time to detect the notes and chords you play. It is never recorded, saved or sent anywhere. Android asks for the permission the first time you turn listening on; if you say no, playback, the editor and everything else still work.

Play nearby (local multiplayer)

Play nearby lets you jam or duel with friends whose phones are in the same room. Phones connect directly to each other over your local Wi-Fi network or, only if you turn on the Bluetooth switch in the Play nearby lobby (off by default), over Bluetooth using Google's Nearby Connections. Nothing goes through the internet or to us.

Separately, for the update check: once a day the app checks for a newer version by asking the Magmanet server (EU, Contabo) — the request contains only the app name, its version number and how it was installed (site/Google Play); the server counts checks per app and version in aggregate, stores no identifiers and no IP address; if the server is unavailable the app downloads the public version file from GitHub Pages instead (GitHub may see your IP address). If a newer version exists, the app shows a notice and you choose whether to open the download link. You can turn this off in Settings ("Check for updates automatically").

  • What the other players see: the display name and emoji you choose, plus game data: the piece, part and speed, timing, and scores. There is no chat. Your imported pieces are never sent.
  • Wi-Fi permissions: the app uses the network permissions only to find and connect to the other phones on the same network.
  • Bluetooth and location permissions: on some Android versions, Android requires the location permission (and, on newer versions, the "Nearby devices" permission) before an app may search for nearby Bluetooth devices. TabMaster asks for these only when you turn the Bluetooth switch on. The app never reads, stores or sends your location. If you say no, Play nearby still works over Wi-Fi.

Anonymous usage statistics

TabMaster sends anonymous usage statistics over HTTPS (a short ping once a day; usage events a few times a day at most, batched) to Magmanet's own server, hosted in the EU (Contabo), not to Google or any other third party. It sends the app name and version, your Android version, your phone's manufacturer and model (such as Samsung SM-S911B; never the name you gave the phone), your language, how the app was installed (from Google Play, the website or elsewhere), a random install ID, and anonymous usage events (which screens and features are used and for how long). The random ID is made by the app when it is installed; it is not a device identifier. No personal content is sent.

On the server the ID is hashed and no IP address is stored. Raw data is deleted after 400 days. It is used only to count active users and improve the app, never for advertising or profiling, and it is never sold or shared.

This is on by default. You can turn it off in Settings ("Share anonymous usage statistics"). Turning it off stops sending immediately and deletes the random ID and any unsent usage events from your phone.
