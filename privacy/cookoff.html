Privacy Policy — Dinner Duel (מי מבשל הכי טוב?)

Last updated: 2026-11-21

Dinner Duel (מי מבשל הכי טוב?) is made by Magmanet. This policy explains what the app does with your information. The short version: your seasons and cook-offs are stored on the Magmanet server so that the people you invite can see them, and only they can; your scores stay sealed until the finale; you only need a nickname; the app also sends anonymous usage statistics, which you can turn off.

What we collect

  • Your groups (dinner seasons and one-night cook-offs), because hosting and scoring together is what the app does: the season name and finale day, dinner dates, who hosts each evening, menu teasers (starter, main, dessert), themes and notes for guests, cook-off cooks and dish names, each member's scores (1 to 10 per category) and best-dish vote.
  • Your nickname (2–16 characters, chosen by you; no real name needed), shown to the members of your groups.
  • Anonymous usage statistics (see below), which you can turn off.

The app does not ask for an email address, a phone number, your contacts, photos or your location. There is no advertising and no tracking. We never sell or share your data.

Who can see your groups

Only the members of each group. A group starts with the person who creates it (the organizer); others join only with the group's invite code, which members choose to send (for example on WhatsApp). Members see the group, the other members' nicknames, the dinners and menus, and how many guests have scored each dinner. Scores and votes are sealed: until the season finale (or until the organizer reveals the results of a cook-off), the server sends each member only their own scores and vote, the organizer and the hosts included. After the finale, all members see everyone's scores and votes. Nobody else can read a group: there are no public groups, no search and no profiles. The organizer can remove a member and make a new invite code so the old one stops working. Menus and cook-off entries can be reported by members; after three reports one is hidden.

Where your data is stored

  • On your phone: a copy of your groups (so they open offline), changes waiting to be sent, your nickname and settings, in the app's private storage. They are removed when you uninstall the app. The Copy backup feature (Settings → Your data) copies this to your clipboard as text; where you paste it is up to you.
  • On the Magmanet server: your groups and your nickname in them, run by Magmanet on a server hosted in the European Union (by Contabo). It is not sent to Google or any other third party and is not used for advertising or profiling. Data is encrypted in transit (HTTPS).

The account is anonymous: the server creates a random user id and access tokens, stored on your phone. The app also stores a random number made at install time (not a device identifier), which it sends when the account is created so the server can limit abuse. Other members never see these ids; everything shows by nickname only.

How the app talks to the server

There are no notifications. While a group is open the app asks the server for changes every few seconds, and your groups are refreshed when you open the app or come back to it (and when you pull down to refresh). Without a connection, your scores and edits wait on the phone and are sent when you are back online.

How long, and how to delete

  • Leave the group (group menu): you are removed from it and your scores and vote are deleted; dinners and cook-off entries you added stay unless you delete them first.
  • Delete a dinner or entry: the person who added it (or the organizer) can delete it.
  • Delete for everyone (organizer): the group, all dinners, scores and votes are deleted from the server for everyone.
  • Delete all my data (Settings): you leave every group and your anonymous account is deleted from the server.
  • Groups nobody has touched for 400 days are deleted automatically; deleted items are kept for up to 30 days so syncing works, then removed.
  • Uninstalling the app does not delete your groups on the server (the anonymous account can't be reached any more), so leave your groups or use "Delete all my data" first, or contact us (below) with the group's name and your nickname and we will remove them.

Internet use

The app connects to the Magmanet server to sync your groups. Once a day it also checks for a newer version by asking the Magmanet server (EU, Contabo) — the request contains only the app name, its version number and how it was installed (site/Google Play); the server counts checks per app and version in aggregate, stores no identifiers and no IP address; if the server is unavailable the app downloads the public version file from GitHub Pages instead (GitHub may see your IP address). If a newer version exists, the app shows a notice and you choose whether to open the download link. You can turn this off in Settings ("Check for updates automatically").

The invite message you share contains the group's name (or the cook-off's dish), the invite code and a link to the app's page on the Magmanet apps site. Menu cards, results cards and theme ideas you share are made on your phone. They only go where you send them.

Anonymous usage statistics

Dinner Duel sends anonymous usage statistics over HTTPS (a short ping once a day; usage events a few times a day at most, batched) to Magmanet's own server, hosted in the EU (Contabo), not to Google or any other third party. It sends the app name and version, your Android version, your phone's manufacturer and model (such as Samsung SM-S911B; never the name you gave the phone), your language, how the app was installed (from Google Play, the website or elsewhere), a random install ID, and anonymous usage events (which screens are used and for how long). The random ID is made by the app when it is installed; it is not a device identifier. No personal content is sent with the statistics: no names, menus, scores or nicknames.

On the server the ID is hashed and no IP address is stored. Raw data is deleted after 400 days. It is used only to count active users and improve the app, never for advertising or profiling, and it is never sold or shared.

This is on by default. You can turn it off in Settings ("Share anonymous usage statistics"). Turning it off stops sending immediately and deletes the random ID and any unsent usage events from your phone.

Permissions

  • Internet: syncing your groups with the Magmanet server, the daily update check and the anonymous usage statistics.

The app has no access to your contacts, photos, camera, microphone or location, and sends no notifications. The clipboard is read only when you tap "Paste" on the join screen.

Children

Dinner Duel is a general-audience app for groups of friends and families and is not directed at children.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update the date above and publish the new version at the same address.
