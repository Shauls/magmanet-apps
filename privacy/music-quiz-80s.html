Privacy Policy — 80s Music Quiz

Last updated: 2026-10-15

80s Music Quiz is made by Magmanet. This policy explains what the app does with your information. The short version: your data stays on your device.

What we collect

We don't collect anything. 80s Music Quiz has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. We never see, sell or share your data.

Where your data is stored

Everything you enter is saved only on your device, in the app's private storage. It is removed when you uninstall the app.

The app's Copy backup feature (Settings → Your data) copies your data as text to your clipboard, so you can keep it somewhere you choose. Where you paste it is up to you; the app never uploads it.

Internet use

Most of the app works offline. The listening games need the internet, because they play official 30-second song previews from Apple. These features contact Apple directly from your device:

  • Guess the Tune and Intro Rush: look up previews for several songs before the round starts.
  • Snippet and Daily Snippet: look up the preview for the song you are guessing (and, in endless Snippet, the next song in advance).
  • Buzzer Party: looks up previews for the round.
  • Play nearby → Nearby tune: only the host phone looks up and plays the previews; the other phones receive just the song choices over the local network.
  • Song pages (opened from an artist page or a "Did you know?" card): look up the preview only when you tap the play button.

What is sent: each lookup is a request to the Apple iTunes Search API (itunes.apple.com) containing the artist and song title of a song from the app's built-in list. The preview audio is then streamed from Apple's servers. Nothing you type is sent (the artist and song search boxes only filter the built-in list on your device), and no name, account or other personal data is included. Previews are streamed, not saved.

Like any internet request, Apple can see your device's IP address. We (the developer) receive nothing: no copy of these requests, no IP address and no usage data. Apple's privacy policy: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/

Separately, at most once a day the app checks whether a newer version is available by downloading a small public version file from GitHub Pages (shauls.github.io). Nothing about you or your device is sent; like any web request, GitHub can see your device's IP address. If a newer version exists, the app shows a notice and you choose whether to open the download link. You can turn this off in Settings ("Check for updates automatically").

Links: after you answer, the Open in Apple Music button (on listening screens and song pages) opens that track's page on Apple Music (music.apple.com) in the Apple Music app or your browser. This happens only when you tap it, and Apple's privacy policy applies there.

Read aloud: the read-aloud option uses your device's own text-to-speech engine. The app does not send the text anywhere.

Play nearby

Play nearby lets several phones in the same room play together. It connects the phones directly to each other over your Wi-Fi network (local network). Nothing goes through the internet or through our servers.

The phones exchange only what the game needs: your display name, your emoji avatar, and the game itself (questions, answers, buzzes and scores). This is shared only with the phones in that game and is not saved by the app after the game ends.

There is an optional Also use Bluetooth switch in the nearby lobby. It is off by default. When you turn it on, the app uses Google Nearby Connections to find phones over Bluetooth and nearby Wi-Fi, and Android asks you for these permissions:

  • Bluetooth and Nearby devices (Bluetooth scan, advertise and connect, nearby Wi-Fi devices): to find and connect to the other phones.
  • Location (approximate and precise): some Android versions require location permission before an app can scan for Bluetooth devices. The app never reads, stores or sends your location.

If you say no or leave the switch off, Play nearby still works over Wi-Fi.

Permissions

The app asks for a permission only when you use the feature that needs it. You can say no and still use the rest of the app.

  • Internet and network state: to stream song previews, to check the connection, and for the daily update check.
  • Wi-Fi state and multicast: to find other phones on the same Wi-Fi for Play nearby.
  • Bluetooth, Nearby devices and Location: only for the optional Bluetooth switch in Play nearby, as explained above. Location is never read or used.
  • Opening links: if you choose to open a link, it opens in Apple Music or your browser, which have their own privacy policies.
